I recently attended the annual convention of the Connecticut Association of Boards of Education (CABE).  One of the best sessions described how a small Eastern Connecticut school district remade itself.

A few years ago, the school's superintendent and principal would barely speak to one another.  The teachers, board, and entire community was divided into Team Supe and Team Principal, and the students were suffering.  It was total dysfunction.  They had to do something to fix this toxic climate.

The BOE took the rare and risky steps of removing both of the administrators at once, hiring two new ones, and elevating a very young new Board Chair (she couldn't even legally drink!)  They brought in consultants from CABE and the Superintendent worked extremely closely with the Board and CABE to create a more hospitable workplace and a relentless focus on what is best for the students.  They offered generous buyouts for staff members unable to adapt

It worked.  At this convention, the Board received the CABE award for excellence -- one of only eight Boards in the state.  (And a shoutout to RHAM, which also received one.)  Student scores are now on the rise, and there's no infighting at all. The right people with the right focus were able to turn things around.

Let me turn to Andover.  Andover Elementary school has had some fantastic academic news recently.  Probably the most important metric the state collects for schools is the proportion of the individualized academic goals its students meet.  This number is important to schools because the students' individual differences are smoothed over in the measurement, and it reports how well the school itself is doing.

Andover had the best record in the state in 2025-26.  By a lot. Against a state average score of 60.1, Andover reached 84.9.  Only one other town passed 80, Westbrook at 80.3.  A full report is at andoverct.info/reports/aes/test-results-2025-26/ and the key chart is at andoverct.info/links/102/.  In the same year, Andover had the highest increase in the state in both English and Math scores.

What these two districts have in common?  Superintendent Valerie Bruneau, who works 40% for Scotland, 60% for Andover, and 100% for the kids.  It is not Val alone, of course,  AES has an incredible principal, a focused, enthusiastic staff, and an engaged, supportive Board.  Scotland elected a dynamic Board chair and with CABE's help, has eliminated the toxicity that had been bringing them down.  But the Superintendent sets the tone and the direction.  A lot can be attributed to Val.

This makes Andover's First Selectman's attitude mysterious.  We have one of the best school districts in the state, led by a talented, widely respected administrator.  Why is he continually trying to take over the roles state law and our own charter gives to the BOE and the Superintendent?  It simply makes no sense!

Congratulations, Scotland!  Congratuations, Andover Elementary!  And Congratulations to Dr. Bruneau for leading all these successes.
