In September I wrote here about the librarians who shaped my life, and said that November 3 would give me the chance to hand something back. That day is nearly here, so let me finish the story with what I’ve learned about Ellen Paul since.

She never expected to run for office. She was raised, she says, to believe that citizenship means more than voting, and that when you can make a difference, you step forward. She grew up in Mansfield and lives in Glastonbury with her husband and two daughters, and she has been stepping forward for years.

She spent four years working with a Republican senator, Tony Hwang, on a library e-book bill. When it passed the Senate 35 to 1 in 2025, she said she was “just overjoyed that we finally got it across the finish line.” That same year she helped pass the freedom-to-read law, which she says protects a library’s ability to carry the Bible as much as books about gender identity. In May, Library Journal named her one of its Movers and Shakers.

That is the habit I want in Hartford: find the people who disagree with you, listen, and get the work done anyway.

Her plans are practical. Modernize the school funding formula, untouched since 2013, so property taxes stop covering the gap. Build homes that teachers, nurses and young families can afford. Put ratepayers first on electric bills. Keep our farms and forests, because, as she puts it, some things can be rebuilt but our green places are gone forever once they’re gone. And require legislators to hold regular public town halls in the towns they represent.

She also believes some votes should be easy. Protecting children. Keeping seniors safe. When those bills come up, she promises to ask one question: does this make life better and safer for the people of the 55th? If so, she votes yes.

Early voting runs through Sunday, November 1. On Tuesday, November 3, polls are open 6 a.m. to 8 p.m., and you can even register that day. Everything you need is at myvote.ct.gov.

More than once, a librarian handed me the right book at the right moment. On Tuesday, we can hand one the right job. Please vote for Ellen Paul.
