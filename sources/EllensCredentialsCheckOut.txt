I keep seeing one question about Ellen Paul, the Democratic candidate for state representative in the 55th District: what qualifies a librarian to
serve in Hartford?

The honest answer is that no job qualifies anyone. Connecticut has a citizen legislature. Its members serve part-time and come from ordinary working lives on purpose, so the people writing our laws also live under them. There is no credential for the job, and there shouldn't be.

Our current representative runs a restoration company, owns commercial real estate, and flies helicopters. That is honest work, and I don't doubt he is good at it. But none of it is more training for writing state law than a library degree. The real question is what each person brings.

A good librarian brings a method: find the best information, check it, organize it so others can use it, and listen closely enough to learn what people actually need. She also brings the habit of stretching a thin budget. The Connecticut Library Consortium, which Ellen has led since 2022, gets a state grant of $124,402 a year, down from $350,000 a decade earlier, and estimates that its contracts save libraries and taxpayers over $7 million a year.

And then there is this librarian. Ellen spent four years working with Republican Sen. Tony Hwang, on a library e-book bill, testifying and building a coalition until it passed the Senate 35 to 1 in 2025. Steve Weir voted no. That same year she helped write the freedom-to-read protections that became law in the state budget.

In fairness, individual members' bills rarely pass in Hartford, in either party. Laws get made the way Ellen made hers, through years of work across the aisle. Weir has filed 41 bills of his own in four years, and none reached a floor vote. The clearest case I can find of him steering an outcome came on the last night of the 2026 session, when he and a few allies threatened to run out the clock on SB 274, a tenant-safety bill that had already passed the Senate. It died. He sits on the board of the Connecticut Apartment Association, whose president testified against it.

So what qualifies a librarian to serve in Hartford? She's already done the job's hardest part from outside the building. Weir is kind and hard-working. Ellen is too, and she has two laws to show for it.
