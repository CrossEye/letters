On September 10, I emailed our state representative, Steve Weir, one question: will you publicly disavow Donald Trump? I meant a statement in his own name that Mr. Trump does not represent him and does not have his support. I sent it to the campaign address on his state election filing and copied his legislative office, because I am his constituent. Four other Republican candidates got the same question in the same words.

I know it is a hard question. Mr. Trump is the most divisive politician in memory, and any Republican who answers it will upset somebody. But we do not send representatives to Hartford to handle the easy questions. How a candidate judges the leader of his own party tells voters something about his judgment, and judgment is what we are hiring.

One candidate has already shown it can be answered. Jenn Jennings, the Republican running for the 4th Senate District, on the same Andover ballot as Mr. Weir, replied within three days. Her answer was no, and she made a thoughtful case that it is the wrong question to ask in a state race. I disagree with her conclusion. But she answered plainly, on the record, knowing every word would be published, and that took some courage. Voters now have something real to weigh.

As of this writing, September 28, eighteen days after I asked, I have no reply from Mr. Weir. Nor have I heard from Ryan Fazio, George Austin or Jason Guidone. I draw no conclusion about what any of them thinks; campaign inboxes are busy places. It is a hard question, but it takes only one sentence to answer.

The offer has no deadline. Whatever Mr. Weir sends, in whatever words, I will publish in full and unedited beside my own letter at andoverct.info/reports/ahm/disavowal. I am a Democrat and co-chair the Andover Democratic Town Committee, and readers should weigh that. They can also check every word of the record for themselves.

Mr. Weir is asking Andover, Bolton, Glastonbury, Hebron and Marlborough for another term. One sentence from him would settle this, whichever way it goes.
