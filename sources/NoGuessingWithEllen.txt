He shows up. He listens. He works hard. He cares. He listens. He's honest. He reads every bill. He listens to everyone. He works hard. He shows up.

That is the case for Steve Weir as his supporters have made it in these pages, and I'm sure it's true. It is a great deal of praise. But something is missing.

On Sept. 4, Chesney McOmber of Andover counted eight letters praising Mr. Weir since mid-July and found that not one defended a single vote he had cast. That seemed like an invitation his supporters would take up, so I counted what came next. From that issue through last week's, 23 letters praised him. One names a vote and defends it: Cheryl Lage's, on this spring's budget adjustment. The other 22 do not mention a vote at all.

Over the same four weeks, ten letters critical of him named specific votes: the budget, safety fences around pools, ending child marriage, early childhood funding, renewable energy. The letters for him named his family, his farm, his church, his business, his scooter, his helicopter. Nothing in the second list is wrong. It's just not what we send a representative to Hartford to do.

So the only people discussing his record are his critics. His supporters know him far better than I do, and they are not shy; they have filled page after page. If there were votes they were proud of, I think they would tell us. Their silence tells us something too.

The closest thing to a record in all 23 letters is a single number. James DeDonato of Hebron wrote that Mr. Weir votes with the majority 77 percent of the time, offered as proof that he works across the aisle. It sounds reassuring until you compare it with his colleagues: over his full term, only seven of the 154 people who served in the House voted with the majority less often.

Mr. Weir is plainly a good neighbor. Ms. McOmber's invitation still stands: pick a vote and make the case for it. Ellen Paul already has. Her website goes issue by issue through the bills that have reached the House, on schools, housing, energy, and reproductive care, and says how she will vote when they come back. As she says, "you won't have to guess." That is the representative I want, and I will vote for her on Nov. 3.
