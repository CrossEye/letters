Last week's Rivereast carried seven letters for Steve Weir, five from Hebron and two from Marlborough. They describe a man who answers his phone, helps a neighbor corral loose cows, and stepped in for a young soldier after his father died. I do not doubt a word of it.

Six of the seven do not name a single vote he has cast.

The seventh does, and I want to thank Cheryl Lage of Amston for it. She is the first Weir supporter I have seen take up an actual vote and make a case for it. Her account is that the budget landed after midnight, hundreds of pages long, carrying items that had never been properly debated, so he voted no. She adds that he was not voting against our towns getting the money, that he was in favor of it.

That last part is where it comes apart. A legislator has one way to be in favor of something, and it is the vote. Take her account as given: 48 Republicans faced the same stack of paper under the same clock, and 27 voted yes. The bill passed 127 to 21 and carried $630,000 in new aid to the five 55th district towns. It came home because they voted yes.

Reasons explain a vote. They do not stand in for it. Nearly every bill is a package, and every member weighs the parts they dislike against the rest. That weighing is not a technicality that gets a vote out from under him. It is the vote. On this one he decided his objections outweighed $630,000 for his own towns.

Ms. Lage also suggests that anyone troubled by a vote should call and ask. Fair, and people have. David Olio of Amston wrote here two weeks ago that he had several pleasant conversations with our representative.  The votes came out no all the same.

A correction of my own: in August I wrote that 28 of 49 Republicans backed that budget. One member sat in the wrong caucus in my data. The right count is 27 of 48.  I apologize.

One letter noted that several Weir critics are active in town committees. True, the paper prints that under my name every time; party work is why I have the roll calls at hand. 

More letters are welcome. Pick a vote and make the case for it.
