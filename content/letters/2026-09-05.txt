Let me tell you about librarians.

Mrs. Hatch, who ran my elementary school library, taught us to read stories
aloud and had a gift for putting the right book in the right hands at the right
moment. Some of you have heard me tell of her before. I make no apologies. She
earned it.

A junior town librarian of my childhood broke a rule for me. Adult cards began
at thirteen, and then only with parents' permission; I was ten and had read my
way through the children's room. She glanced around to see that no one was
looking, then issued the card. In gratitude I tried to teach her to juggle. I
was probably a terrible teacher at ten. I hope she kept practicing.  I am
ashamed to say I no longer remember her name.  I have never forgotten the card.

In high school, Miss K taught me what research actually is: not finding an
answer but testing one. Where did this come from? Who wrote it, and what did
they want me to believe? What would it take to prove it wrong? I have leaned on
those questions my entire adult life.

So when I learned a librarian was running for State Representative in the 55th
District, I was delighted even before I read a word of her platform. Then I read
the record. Ellen Paul leads the Connecticut Library Consortium, helping more
than a thousand libraries of every kind pool their buying power. And in 2025 she
helped pass two state laws: the e-book pricing law, Public Act 25-9, four years
of coalition work with a Republican senator among its lead champions, and the
freedom-to-read law, Public Act 25-168, which protects a library's ability to
carry the Bible as surely as any book someone else would remove.

Our current representative voted against the e-book bill, and against the budget
act carrying the freedom-to-read provisions.

Ellen Paul is a candidate who has never held office but spent years on
Congressman Joe Courtney's staff, who ran the East Hampton library when it won a
statewide excellence award, and who, as a legislative outsider, helped deliver
two state laws. A librarian on the ballot! I can't stop asking myself, "How did
we get so lucky?"

I have spent a lifetime being handed the right book at the right moment. On
November 3, I get the chance to hand something back.  ellenforstaterep.com/
