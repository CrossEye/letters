Sam Prentice says he has tried to understand what Democrats stand for.  John
Barnowski can speak for himself; I’ll explain what Ellen Paul and I said.

Our most important issue, he said?  This fall I’ve written about town aid, pool
fences, tenant safety, nursing homes and wage theft.  My Banned Books Week
letter turned halfway to hospital investors and absentee landlords.

He calls it outrageous that Ellen’s law “protects a library’s ability to carry
books like the Bible as much as it protects books about gender identity.”  That
is not moral equivalence.  It just means the same rules apply to both.  The law
bars removing a book because of its author’s views.  That guards his shelf
against those who dislike his views exactly as it guards other shelves against
him.

In 2023 a Utah school district, acting on one person’s challenge, pulled the
Bible from school shelves for vulgarity and violence.  Anyone who has read
Genesis 19, 2 Samuel 11, or Judges 19 knows what the reviewers found.  Those
chapters don’t make the Bible unfit for a library.  They show why no single
objector should decide what is on the shelf for everyone else’s child.

He compares book review to movie ratings.  But ratings don’t pull a film from
every theater because one patron objects.  They inform parents, and families
decide. Connecticut’s law works the same way: every library needs a written
collection policy, and anyone objecting to a title gets a fair, consistent
process.

Nor is raising the marriage age a contradiction.  Marriage binds a child to an
adult by contract.  A library book binds no one, and a parent can return it.
The Senate passed that law unanimously, the House 98 to 45, and Connecticut
became the ninth state to end child marriage.  Rep. Steve Weir voted no.

I've repeatedly debunked his minumum wage theory; no need to do so again.

Why do Democrats care about children not their own? Caring for other people’s
children is the whole premise of public schools and libraries.  As for education
at six months, whatever he means, the 2025 early childhood bill passed the House
133 to 10. Weir was one of the ten.

He closes by wishing for more children and fewer Democrats.  I’d settle for more
children in well-funded schools, choosing from shelves filled by parents and
librarians, not the loudest objector.  On Nov. 3, that means Ellen Paul.
