Title: Weir At Your Disservice
Date: 2026-07-10
Topics: Steve Weir, 55th District
Content:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every so often Hartford dreams up a scheme so radical that only a true
conservative can stop it.  This spring it was House Bill 5467: a plan to help
small businesses cut through state red tape by giving them a single person to
call at the economic-development office.  A concierge, essentially, for the shop
owner drowning in permits and paperwork.

The House passed it 143 to 4.

You will not be surprised to learn who supplied one of those four votes.  Our
representative, Steve Weir, looked at a bill designed to make state government
less of a nuisance for small business and decided this was the hill.  Forty-five
of his fellow Republicans voted yes.  Every Democrat voted yes.  Steve voted no.
Four members of the entire chamber stood against it, and he was one of them.

I have tried, as I always do, to reconstruct the principle.  This is a man whose
own biography calls him a small-business owner, recent chair of the CT River
Valley Chamber of Commerce, and a "staunch supporter of free markets" and
"limiting government intrusion."  If you drew a Venn diagram of the people who
should have sprinted to the microphone to vote yes, Steve Weir would be the
overlap.  He is the concierge's ideal customer.

And yet.  Perhaps he found the state being too helpful.  Perhaps a man who has
navigated the permits himself resents the thought of anyone else getting a
guide.  Perhaps "limiting government intrusion" turns out to include the parts 
of government that intrude by helping.

Whatever the reason, he has not shared it, and the vote is on the record either
way.

When 147 of your colleagues, from both parties, agree that the small businesses
of Connecticut deserve a phone number to call, and you are one of four who says
no, the explaining is not their job.  It is yours.

So I would like to hear it.  Not a slogan about free markets; those he has in
abundance.  An actual reason the businesses of the 55th District should not have
gotten this small, unglamorous help.  Forty-five Republicans managed to vote for
it. Our representative couldn't.
