Title: Officials May Argue. Offices Should Not
Date: 2026-07-17
Topics: Andover, Andover Charter
Content:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I write as an Andover resident, about Andover's charter, though the question is
one any town in the area might recognize.

When a town budget, a bond, or any other question reaches the ballot, the
decision belongs to the voters. Our elected officials are free to argue their
side, and we are better off hearing them. What should not happen is for the
weight of an office, rather than the force of an argument, to land on one side
of a question the public has reserved for itself. A selectman may oppose a
budget. The Board of Selectmen should not.

Chapter II of Andover's charter governs elections, and runs through Section 209.
I would offer this for a future charter revision as a new Section 210, written
to be read by anyone and not only by lawyers:

> **Section 210. Official Neutrality on Questions Before the Voters**
> 
> A. Once a question has been placed before the electors of Andover, no board
> established or governed under this Charter, and no member acting in an official
> capacity, shall invoke the name, title, or authority of that board to urge a
> vote for or against the question.
> 
> B. Nothing in this section limits any official from speaking as a private
> citizen, provided the official makes clear that the view is personal and is not
> the position of the board on which they serve.
> 
> C. Town funds, staff, equipment, and official communication channels shall not
> be used to advocate for or against any such question. Nothing in this section
> prevents a board or official from encouraging residents to vote, or from
> publishing impartial factual information about the question and about how and
> when to vote.

This takes no one's voice away. It asks only that a public office stay on a
decision the public has kept for itself, whoever holds the office and whichever
side they favor.
