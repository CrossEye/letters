At the recent Connecticut Association of Boards of Education (CABE) convention,
one of the best sessions described how a small Eastern Connecticut school
district remade itself.

A few years ago, the district's superintendent and principal barely spoke to
each other. Teachers, the board, and the whole community had split into Team
Supe and Team Principal, and the students were suffering.

The Board of Education took the rare and risky step of replacing both
administrators at once, and elevated a very young new chair (she couldn't
legally drink!). With help from CABE consultants, the new superintendent worked
closely with the Board to build a healthier workplace and a relentless focus on
what is best for students. Staff unable to adapt were offered generous buyouts.

It worked. At this convention, that Board received CABE's award for excellence,
one of only eight in the state. (A shoutout to RHAM, which also won one.)
Student scores are rising, and the infighting is gone.

Now to Andover. Perhaps the most important measure the state collects is the
share of students' individualized academic goals a school meets. Because it
measures each student against their own goals, it reflects how well the school
itself is doing.

In 2025-26, Andover Elementary led the state, by a lot: 84.9, against a state
average of 60.1. Only one other town passed 80 (Westbrook, at 80.3). Andover
also had the state's largest gains in both English and math. The full report is
at andoverct.info/reports/aes/test-results-2025-26/. The key chart is at
andoverct.info/links/102/.

What do these two districts have in common? Superintendent Valerie Bruneau, who
works 40% for Scotland, 60% for Andover, and 110% for the kids. Val doesn't do
it alone, of course. AES has an incredible principal, a focused, enthusiastic
staff, and an engaged, supportive Board. But the superintendent sets the tone
and direction, and much of the credit belongs to Val.

That makes our First Selectman's attitude mysterious. We have one of the best
school districts in the state with one of the lowest per-pupil costs, led by a
talented, widely respected administrator. Why does he keep trying to take over
roles that state law and our own charter give to the Board of Education and the
Superintendent?

Congratulations, Scotland! Congratulations, Andover Elementary! And
congratulations to Dr. Bruneau for leading both successes.
