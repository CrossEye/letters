On July 1, Andover's First Selectman decided, by himself, to change how the
elementary school receives money the voters already approved. For three years
the Board of Finance had released the school's budget in ten equal monthly
payments, July - April, adopted the way boards adopt things: on an agenda, in
public, by a recorded vote. Last July that vote was 7 to 0.

A single email replaced it. Under his scheme, the money would follow only as the
school submitted weekly spending registers for the town's approval. No vote of
the Selectmen. No vote of the Finance board. One official, one email.

On July 22, the town's own auditor was asked how a change like this should be
made. His answer: by a policy adopted by the Board of Finance. By vote.

So where is the vote?

Under legal threat, the First Selectman soon abandoned this approach. But this
was not an isolated act. The same official reopened a solar contract the school
had already signed so the town could be written in. He repeatedly demanded
preschool records that federal privacy law forbids the school to release. He
mailed every household three flyers urging No votes on the school budget, signed
with his name, never his title. And more: I've documented ten separate episodes,
each from the town's own minutes and recordings.

Here is why it matters: Connecticut law provides that each town shall, through
its board of education, maintain control of its public schools. The town holds
title; the elected board, by statute, has the care, maintenance, and operation
of the buildings and the lands used for school purposes. Our charter lets the
First Selectman sit with other boards to coordinate, not to command. A
Connecticut Supreme Court case puts it plainly: a board of education is beyond
the control of the town or any of its officers in the powers the legislature
gave it (*Board of Education v. Board of Finance*, 127 Conn. 345 (1940)).

The letter of the law is the vote he skipped. The spirit is a school board the
voters elected, quietly displaced by one man who would rather run the school
himself.

I have laid out all of it, every claim tied to documents you can check, at
andoverct.info/series/town-asset. His flyers cited nothing.

Read it, check anything you doubt, and then ask the question the auditor's
answer leaves hanging.

Where is the vote?
