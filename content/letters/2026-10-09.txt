Title: Ellen Protects the Shelves
Date: 2026-10-09
Topics: Ellen Paul, Steve Weir, 55th District, Voting Record, Landlords
TeaserParagraphs: 2
Content:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is Banned Books Week, a good time to ask a simple question about the 55th
District: whose freedoms does our representative protect?

Ellen Paul's answer is on the public record. In 2025 she helped write and pass
Connecticut's freedom-to-read law, which bars libraries from removing a book
because of the origin or views of its author, gives anyone who objects to a
title a fair and consistent process, and protects librarians who act in good
faith. In her words, it "protects a library's ability to carry books like the
Bible as much as it protects books about gender identity." That same year she
helped pass the library e-book law, with a Republican senator, Tony Hwang, among
its lead champions. It cleared the Senate 35 to 1.

State Rep. Steve Weir voted against the ebook bill. He also voted against the
2025 budget act that carried the freedom-to-read provisions. In fairness, that
was a vote on the whole budget, and he would say it was about spending. So look
at his votes where spending was not the question.

This spring he voted against oversight of private-equity ownership of hospitals,
though most of his own caucus voted 'yes.' He voted against stronger wage-theft
enforcement, and against limits on warehouse productivity quotas. He voted
against nursing-home transparency that his fellow Republicans favored 41 to 10.
And when a bill to hold out-of-state apartment owners accountable after the
Rocky Hill apartment disaster came up on the session's last night, having passed
the Senate and cleared committee without a single 'no' vote, he and a few allies
threatened to run out the clock until leadership pulled it. He sits on the board
of the Connecticut Apartment Association, the landlord trade group whose
president testified against it.

Hospital investors, employers who shortchange paychecks, absentee landlords.
Weir is a hard-working man, but that is where his votes land. Ellen has spent
her career protecting the right of our children to walk into a library and find
the book they need.

On Nov. 3, I know which kind of protector I want in Hartford.
