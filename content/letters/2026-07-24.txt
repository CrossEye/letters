Title: A Vote Worth Missing
Date: 2026-07-24
Topics: Steve Weir, 55th District, Voting Record
TeaserParagraphs: 2
Content:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

State Rep. Steve Weir shared some news on Facebook this month: in his four years
at the Capitol, he has never missed a vote. All 650 recorded votes of the term,
and he was present for every one. That is true, and it deserves credit. It is
also true of Speaker Ritter, of our neighbor Rep. Tim Ackert, and of 35 other
members of the House, none of whom made a graphic about it. Showing up is the
job, and roughly a quarter of the chamber does it perfectly. But grant him this:
he shows up.

Which is just the trouble with a perfect attendance record. It means every vote
is a choice. No gaps, no sick days, no "I missed that one." When our
representative tells us he has never missed a vote, he is telling us that
everything on his roll call was done on purpose.

Including June of 2023.

That month the House took up House Bill 6569, raising Connecticut's minimum
marriage age to 18, with no exceptions. It ended child marriage in this state
and made us the ninth state in the nation to do so. The Senate had passed it
unanimously: every Democrat, every Republican, not a single dissent. The House
passed it 98 to 45, and the governor signed it into law.

Steve Weir voted 'no.'

The bill did one thing. It said that children cannot marry in Connecticut.
Senators of both parties found it an easy yes, all of them. Our representative
read the same page and found his way to 'no,' and thanks to his diligence we
know it was not an accident. He was there. He heard the debate. He chose.

Perfect attendance is an honor we mostly give to schoolchildren. Protecting them
would have been the better one to claim.
