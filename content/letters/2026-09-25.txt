There are plenty of reasons to vote for Ellen Paul. Mine is simple: she shares
my values. Everyone says that about their candidate, so let me be specific.

I think a fence around a backyard pool is a small price for a child not
drowning. I think a decision about a pregnancy belongs to a woman and her
doctor, not to the legislature. I think that when an out-of-state owner lets an
apartment complex decay until thousands of tenants have to be moved out, the
town where it sits should at least have known who owned it. And I think that
when the state budget carries about $630,000 in new aid for Andover, Bolton,
Glastonbury, Hebron, and Marlborough, you vote yes.

None of that is radical. When those questions reached the House, most of it
agreed with me: 102 to 45 on pool fences, 117 to 27 on reproductive care, 127 to
21 on the budget. The tenant bill, which had passed the Senate and cleared
committee without a single no vote, never got a House vote at all.

Ellen has said plainly where she stands. Decisions about reproductive health
care belong to women and their doctors. Protecting children and keeping seniors
safe should be the easiest votes a legislator ever casts. Our teachers, nurses,
and young families need homes they can afford, and the state's school funding
formula, untouched since 2013, needs fixing so our property taxes stop covering
the gap.

And she is a librarian, for heaven's sake. Librarians belong in the same company
as schoolteachers and nurses: the people who hold a town together, for modest
pay, because they believe in the work. Ellens's values turned the East Hampton
library into an award winner, then took her to the nonprofit whose contracts
libraries in every Connecticut town use to purchase together, and through four
years of coalition work with a Republican senator to pass the state's 2025
library e-book law. Of the second law she helped pass that year, on the freedom
to read, she said it protects a library's ability to carry the Bible as much as
books about gender identity. That is the temperament I want in Hartford.

I said she shares my values. I don't think they are only mine. They are not
Democratic values or Republican values; in these five towns they are simply
ours. Vote for Ellen Paul on November 3.  www.ellenforstaterep.com
